# Global Macro Overview (2026-05-23)

## Current State (May 2026)
The global macroeconomic landscape is currently dominated by a "Geopolitical-Energy Shock" regime, primarily driven by conflicts in the Middle East (specifically strikes involving Iran) and the structural transition of the AI industry.

### 1. Central Bank Policies & Monetary Stance
| Region | Current Rate | Stance | Key Driver | Outlook |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **USA (Fed)** | 3.50% - 3.75% | Wait-and-See | Energy-induced inflation vs. Labor stability | Cuts delayed; potential for 1-2 cuts in late 2026/early 2027. |
| **EU (ECB)** | 2.00% | Tightening Bias | Severe energy shock (CPI spike >11% expected) | Potential hikes in June/Sept 2026 to combat energy inflation. |
| **Japan (BoJ)**| 0.75% | Normalizing | Wage growth & economic recovery | Gradual hikes expected in H2 2026. |
| **China (PBoC)**| Easing | Supportive | Weak domestic demand, property slump | Continued policy easing to support 4.7% GDP growth. |

### 2. Key Macro Indicators
- **Inflation**: Headline inflation is spiking globally due to energy. US PCE is around 2.8%. EU inflation is seeing a sharp surge.
- **Bond Yields**: US 10Y Treasury is trading around 4.5-4.6%. Yield curves are generally positive but sensitive to energy price volatility.
- **GDP Growth**: US remains resilient (2.3% forecast). Euro Area slowing (1.1% forecast) due to energy imports. China stable at ~4.7%.

### 3. The "AI Agent" Paradigm Shift
The industry is transitioning from **Model Training** $\rightarrow$ **Inference/Agentic Application**.
- **Capex**: CSPs (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) projected to spend ~$716B.
- **Demand**: Token generation demand is exploding as AI moves from "information provider" to "task completer".
- **Economic Impact**: Long-term productivity gains are expected to act as a disinflationary force, potentially allowing central banks to be more dovish in the long run despite short-term shocks.

### 4. Geopolitical Risk Factors
- **Middle East Conflict**: Primary driver of oil price volatility (Brent ~$90-$110).
- **US Trade Policy**: Tariffs and strategic shifts continue to create supply-side volatility.
- **Energy Security**: EU's vulnerability to energy disruptions is creating a divergence in growth between the US (energy exporter) and EU (energy importer).
