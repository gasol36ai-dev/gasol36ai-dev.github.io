# Macro Intelligence Report - 2026-05-12

## 1. Executive Summary
The global macro environment is currently defined by a "Hawkish Hold" regime. While major central banks (Fed, ECB, BoE) have maintained rates in their April meetings, the signaling has shifted significantly toward hawkishness due to escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East (specifically Iran-related risks) and the resulting energy price shocks. The outlook has become "binary": either a material de-escalation in hostilities leads to the expected rate cuts later this year, or persistent energy-led inflation forces renewed tightening.

## 2. Central Bank Policy & Divergence
### Federal Reserve (Fed)
- **Status:** Hold (Fed Funds Rate: 3.5% - 3.75%).
- **Signal:** Binary. Chair Powell left the door open for cuts, but internal dissents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) oppose an easing bias.
- **Outlook:** Base case remains two cuts (Sept, Dec), but conditional on energy prices pulling back by late May.
- **Key Variable:** Iran-related inflation risks.

### European Central Bank (ECB)
- **Status:** Hold (Deposit Facility Rate: 2.00%).
- **Signal:** Hawkish. "Standing ready" to adjust instruments. Markets price a June hike (90% prob), though some institutions (State Street) believe the ECB can look through the supply shock.
- **Outlook:** Risk of June 11 hike if second-round effects emerge.

### Bank of Japan (BoJ)
- **Status:** Hawkish Hold (Policy Rate: 0.75%).
- **Signal:** Very Hawkish. Record number of dissents for a hike. Policy is now "conditional rather than directional."
- **Outlook:** High probability of two hikes this year; potentially one in June.
- **Key Variable:** Yen weakness and core CPI passthrough.

### Bank of England (BoE)
- **Status:** Hold (Policy Rate: 3.75%).
- **Signal:** Active Hold. Heightened likelihood of tightening due to Middle East instability.
- **Outlook:** Higher probability of rate hikes this year than previously priced.

## 3. Quantitative Data & Trends
- **US Treasury Yields:** Rising across the curve. 30Y above 5% for the first time since July 2025. 10Y around 4.41%.
- **Inflation:**
    - **US:** Short-term inflation expectations increasing (NY Fed Survey: 1-year ahead at 3.6%).
    - **Australia:** Headline CPI jumped to 4.6% YoY in March, driven by fuel. Broadening second-round effects.
- **Energy:** Oil prices surpassed $108/bbl.
- **Growth:** US Real GDP (Q1) at 2.0% SAAR, showing resilience but divergence (Strong IP/Equipment investment vs. shrinking residential).

## 4. Geopolitical Risk & Capital Flows
- **Iran Conflict:** The primary driver of current macro volatility. The "Strait of Hormuz red line" is the critical trigger for a systemic energy shock.
- **Capital Flow:** Flight to quality (USD, Treasuries) during spikes in tension, but long-term yields are rising on inflation fears.
