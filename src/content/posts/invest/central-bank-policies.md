# Central Bank Policy Monitor
## Key Rates, Forward Guidance, and Macro Context
## As of 2026-05-15

## Federal Reserve (USD)
|- **Current Policy Rate**: 5.25-5.50% (unchanged since July 2023)
|- **Latest Move**: Pause in September 2024 after 525bps tightening cycle
|- **Forward Guidance**: 
  - "Higher-for-longer" contingent on inflation progress
  - Markets pricing ~65% probability of no cuts in 2026 (CME Fed Watch)
  - ~25% probability of one hike in 2026
  - Chair Transition: Kevin Warsh (term expires May 15, 2026). Expect potential regime change toward leaner balance sheet/lower rates, but current inflation risks (3.8% YoY April 2026) complicate this.
|- **Balance Sheet**: QT ongoing at ~$25B/month cap
|- **Key Concerns**: 
  - Inflation persistence (CPI 3.8% YoY April 2026 - highest since May 2023)
  - Energy price surge (Oil >$100/bbl) due to Middle East conflict
  - AI-driven capex cycle demand pressures
  - Political/Independence concerns regarding successor (Kevin Warsh)

## European Central Bank (EUR)
|- **Current Deposit Rate**: 2.00% (unchanged April 2026)
|- **Latest Move**: Held steady April 2026 despite inflation rise
|- **Forward Guidance**:
  - Reuters poll: 85% expect 25bp hike to 2.25% in June 2026
  - Nearly 50% expect additional hike later in 2026
  - Data-dependent approach emphasized
|- **Inflation Outlook**: 
  - Headline: ~3.0% YoY April 2026 (energy-driven)
  - Core: Decreasing services inflation offsetting goods rise
  - Long-term expectations anchored ~2.0%
|- **Key Concerns**:
  - War-led energy price shock feeding inflation (Oil >$100/bbl)
  - Growth fragility (Q1 2026: 0.1% QoQ expected; 2026 expansion expected at 0.8%)
  - Stagflation risk: High inflation + slowing growth

## Bank of Japan (JPY)
|- **Current Policy Rate**: 0.75% (raised Dec 2025 from -0.10%)
|- **Latest Move**: Hawkish hold at April meeting
|- **Forward Guidance**:
  - Considering further hike to 1.0% (likely in June 2026)
  - Cautious approach given decades of deflationary mindset
  - Yield curve control adjustments ongoing
|- **Inflation Outlook**:
  - CPI (ex fresh food): 2.0%+ for nearly 4 years
  - Real rates still negative despite nominal hikes
  - Medium/long-term inflation expectations rising
|- **Key Concerns**:
  - Exchange rate volatility (JPY weakness)
  - Global spillovers from BOJ policy normalization
  - Sustainability of wage-price spiral amidst plunging consumer confidence

## Policy Divergence Matrix
| Dimension | Fed | ECB | BoJ |
|-----------|-----|-----|-----|
| **Stance** | Pause/Hawkish | Neutral-to-Hawkish | Dove-to-Neutral |
| **2026 Bias** | Hold → Possible Hike | Hike(s) Expected | Possible Hike |
| **Inflation Driver** | Services/AI capex/Energy | Energy (War) | Persistent (>2%) |
| **Growth Outlook** | Resilient (3.7% GDPNow) | Fragile (Stagflation risk) | Moderating |
| **Key Risk** | Policy error (over/under) | Growth-inflation tradeoff | Yield curve disruption |

## Implications for Asset Allocation
1. **USD Strength Potential**: Divergent Fed/ECB bias favors USD if ECB hikes lag, though Warsh's potential "regime change" could weaken long-term USD dominance.
2. **Carry Trade Opportunities**: BoJ normalization (possible hike in June) may reduce JPY funding appeal, potentially triggering carry unwinds.
3. **Gold & Real Yields**: High inflation (US 3.8%, EU 3.0%) and geopolitical risk (Middle East) support Gold. Monitor US Real Yields closely; if inflation outpaces nominal yield rises, Gold remains highly attractive.
4. **Term Premia Volatility**: Divergent central bank paths increase curve steepening/flattening risks.
5. **Emerging Markets**: USD strength (from Fed hawkishness/ECB lags) poses risk to EM debt and currencies.
