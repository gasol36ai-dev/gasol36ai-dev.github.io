# Global Macroeconomic Outlook - May 2026

## 1. Central Bank Policy & Interest Rates
- **US Federal Reserve**: Maintaining a restrictive stance. Current benchmark rates held steady. Market expectations for 2026 include minimal cuts, with potential hikes returning in H1 2027. Policy remains data-dependent, focusing on core inflation.
- **European Central Bank (ECB)**: Holding rates steady but exhibiting a slight hike bias. Energy shocks resulting from geopolitical tensions in the Middle East are creating persistent upward pressure on headline inflation.
- **Bank of Japan (BoJ)**: Transitioning toward normalization. While rates were held steady in late April 2026, Governor Ueda has signaled a bias for near-term tightening to counter inflationary pressure from surging oil prices (Iran conflict).

## 2. Inflation & Energy Dynamics
- **Inflation Baseline**: Global core CPI is hovering around 3%. US CPI (March 2026) recorded at ~3.26%.
- **The Energy Shock**: The "Strait of Hormuz Blockade" and Iran conflict have pushed Brent crude to $85+ levels. This is creating a structural inflation floor, increasing the risk of second-round effects and wage-price spirals.
- **PCE Metrics**: PCE is trending toward 3.5%, with Core PCE at ~3.2%, driven primarily by energy and associated logistics costs.

## 3. Sovereign Bond Yields & Curve Analysis
- **US 10Y Treasury**: Trading around 4.41% (as of May 5, 2026).
- **Yield Curve**: The 10Y-2Y spread has returned to a positive slope (~50-54bps). This "Normalization" signals a shift from recession fears to an inflation/term-premium regime, typically favoring sector rotation from Mega-Cap Growth into Infrastructure and Energy.

## 4. Geopolitical Risk & Capital Flows
- **Primary Risk**: Middle East conflict (Iran/Strait of Hormuz).
- **Capital Flow Shift**: Increasing "De-dollarization" trends. Central banks are diversifying reserves away from USD-denominated assets toward Gold and other alternatives, creating a structural floor for gold prices.
- **EM Pressure**: Policy divergence (Restrictive Fed vs. Normalizing BoJ/ECB) supports a strong USD, increasing debt servicing costs and capital flight risks for Emerging Market Economies (EMEs).
