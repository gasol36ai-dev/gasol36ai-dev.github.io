# Sovereign Compute Enclave (SCE)

## Definition
A **Sovereign Compute Enclave (SCE)** is a vertically integrated infrastructure unit that collapses the distance between carbon-free energy production and high-density AI compute. Unlike traditional data centers that rely on external utility grids, an SCE is designed for **"Island Mode"** operation, combining a dedicated baseload power source (typically SMRs or Micro-Fusion reactors) with a hyperscale compute fabric.

## Core Architecture
1. **Energy Substrate**: Multi-module Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) or Tritium-based Micro-Fusion (TMF) arrays providing 300MWe+ of dedicated power.
2. **Direct DC-Coupled Fabric**: Elimination of AC/DC conversion losses by coupling the reactor's output directly to the compute power distribution network.
3. **Thermal Recovery Loop**: Use of waste heat from the AI clusters to drive secondary industrial processes or district heating, optimizing total system entropy.
4. **Compute Layer**: High-density GPU/NPU clusters integrated with silicon photonics to minimize interconnect latency and power draw.

## Strategic Value
- **Grid Independence**: Immunity to national grid volatility, brownouts, or geopolitical energy sabotage.
- **Thermal Efficiency**: PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) targets < 1.1 via integrated liquid cooling and direct heat exchange with the reactor coolant.
- **Cognitive Autonomy**: The ability to maintain training and inference cycles regardless of external energy constraints.
