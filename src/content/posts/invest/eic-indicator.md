# Proprietary Indicator: Entropy-Intelligence Coefficient (EIC)

## 1. Overview
The **Entropy-Intelligence Coefficient (EIC)** is a strategic judgment indicator used to assess the viability and required autonomy level of deploying AI assets in extreme, non-deterministic environments. It quantifies the tension between environmental chaos (Entropy) and the system's capacity for decentralized reasoning (Intelligence).

## 2. The Formula
$$EIC = \\frac{H_{env} \\times \\mathcal{R}_{comp}}{I_{edge} \\times S_{resilience}}$$

Where:
- $H_{env}$: **Environmental Entropy Score** (measured via signal-to-noise ratio, thermal variance, and radiation flux).
- $\\mathcal{R}_{comp}$: **Compute Resource Volatility** (availability of localized energy/power).
- $I_{edge}$: **Edge Inference Intelligence** (the complexity of the model that can be run locally, e.g., parameter count vs. latency).
- $S_{resilience}$: **Structural Resilience Factor** (material stability, radiation hardening, and pressure compensation).

## 3. Decision Gates
- **EIC < 1.0 (Stable Deployment):** Standard edge AI deployment. Traditional ruggedization is sufficient.
- **1.0 < EIC < 5.0 (High-Entropy Transition):** Requires EEAS-class hardware. Transition from cloud-reliant to edge-autonomous.
- **EIC > 5.0 (Extreme Frontier):** Requires Neuromorphic, Spiking Neural Networks, and High-Entropy Alloy (HEA) substrates. Deployment is only viable for highly specialized sovereign or exploratory missions.

## 4. Strategic Utility
- **Sovereign Infrastructure:** Used to evaluate the robustness of critical assets (e.g., deep-sea cables, orbital constellations).
- **Investment/Procurement:** Guides the selection of material science vs. software-centric investments in autonomous fleets.
