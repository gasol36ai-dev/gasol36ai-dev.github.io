# 2026 Oil Shock & Macro Divergence Analysis

## Executive Summary
In March 2026, a significant "Iran Oil Shock" disrupted global energy markets, leading to a surge in Brent crude prices (Goldman Sachs forecast: $85/bbl) and a subsequent spike in global inflation. This event has shifted the monetary policy trajectory from a "cautious easing" phase to a "structural inflation" concern, causing divergence among major central banks.

| **BoJ Policy** | < 0.75% | $\uparrow$ (Cautious) | Potential for gradual tightening |

## Transmission Mechanisms

### 1. The Energy-Inflation Loop
$\text{Oil Shock} \rightarrow \uparrow \text{Energy Prices} \rightarrow \uparrow \text{Headline CPI} \rightarrow \text{Fed Pause/Hawkishness} \rightarrow \uparrow \text{Real Yields} \rightarrow \downarrow \text{Gold/Long-Duration Assets}$

### 2. Currency Divergence
The Fed's pause in the face of inflation, contrasted with the ECB's growth concerns and the BoJ's slow ascent, supports a relatively strong **USD Index**. 
- **Strong USD** $\rightarrow$ $\downarrow$ **Emerging Markets (EM)** (due to debt servicing and capital flight).
- **Strong USD** $\rightarrow$ $\downarrow$ **Gold** (inverse correlation).

### 3. Yield Curve Steepening
The transition from an inverted curve to a positive spread (+50bps) indicates that markets are no longer primarily pricing in an immediate recession (the "inverted signal"), but are instead pricing in a **term premium** due to structural inflation and the oil shock.

## Risk Assessment
- **Recession Odds**: J.P. Morgan estimates ~35% probability. The primary risk is a "Hard Landing" if the Fed keeps rates too high for too long to fight oil-driven inflation while growth slows.
- **Stagflation**: High risk for the EU given lower growth projections (0.9%) and persistent energy costs.
