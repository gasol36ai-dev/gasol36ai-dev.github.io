# Research Report: Convergence of Quantum-Resistant Financial Infrastructure and Macro-Regime Detection Models

## Executive Summary
The impending "Quantum Horizon"—the point at which Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computers (CRQC) can execute Shor's algorithm to break asymmetric encryption—represents a systemic risk to global financial stability. The convergence of Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) migration and macro-regime detection models creates a critical inflection point. Financial institutions are not merely upgrading software; they are altering the fundamental trust architecture of the global economy. This transition period is likely to introduce non-linear volatility and liquidity shifts as markets price in "cryptographic obsolescence" and the potential for quantum-enabled information asymmetry.

## 1. Quantum Threat to Financial Cryptographic Primitives
Modern financial infrastructure relies on RSA, ECC, and Diffie-Hellman for secure communication, transaction signing, and identity verification. 
- **The RSA/ECC Collapse:** CRQCs render these algorithms obsolete, potentially enabling the decryption of historical "harvest now, decrypt later" (HNDL) data.
- **Liquidity Infrastructure Risk:** The plumbing of global finance (SWIFT, FedWire, CLS) relies on these primitives. A perceived breakthrough in quantum capability could trigger an immediate "trust vacuum," leading to an abrupt freeze in interbank lending markets.

## 2. Macro-Regime Detection and Quantum Advantage
Macro-regime detection models (e.g., Hidden Markov Models, Gaussian Mixture Models, and deep RL agents) identify shifts in economic states (e.g., from "Growth/Low Vol" to "Recession/High Vol").
- **Information Asymmetry:** A state actor or private entity with early quantum advantage could use Grover's algorithm or quantum-enhanced machine learning (QML) to detect regime shifts significantly faster than classical participants.
- **Signal Distortion:** The migration to PQC involves significant changes in data packet sizes and latency. This "migration noise" may be misinterpreted by classical macro-regime detectors as structural instability, triggering premature hedging or algorithmic sell-offs.

## 3. Impact on Macro Liquidity and Volatility
The transition to quantum resistance is not a seamless patch but a regime shift in itself.
- **Liquidity Fragmentation:** As some jurisdictions move to PQC faster than others, "cryptographic arbitrage" may emerge. Liquidity may migrate toward "safe-haven" PQC-hardened jurisdictions, draining liquidity from lagging emerging markets.
- **Volatility Spikes:** The announcement of a "Quantum Breakthrough" (even if simulated) could trigger a "Quantum Flash Crash." The realization that existing hedges (options/swaps) might be compromised at the clearing-house level would lead to a chaotic repricing of risk.

## 4. Transmission Mappings: [Event] -> [Mechanism] -> [Reaction]

### A. The "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" (HNDL) Reveal
**[Event]** $\rightarrow$ Public disclosure of a CRQC capable of decrypting 10-year-old financial communications.
**[Mechanism]** $\rightarrow$ Revelation of historical insider trading, state-level manipulation, or compromised corporate secrets $\rightarrow$ Collapse of retrospective trust in historical price discovery.
**[Reaction]** $\rightarrow$ Massive volatility in legacy assets; sudden re-evaluation of "fundamental" values based on newly revealed truths.

### B. The PQC Migration Lag
**[Event]** $\rightarrow$ Asynchronous adoption of NIST-standardized PQC across global clearinghouses.
**[Mechanism]** $\rightarrow$ Operational friction in cross-border settlements $\rightarrow$ Increase in settlement failure rates $\rightarrow$ Heightened counterparty risk.
**[Reaction]** $\rightarrow$ Liquidity contraction in the overnight repo market; spike in LIBOR-equivalent spreads.

### C. Quantum-Enhanced Regime Detection (QERD)
**[Event]** $\rightarrow$ Deployment of QML models for macro-trend forecasting.
**[Mechanism]** $\rightarrow$ Quantum actor identifies a regime shift (e.g., impending inflation spike) 100ms before classical models $\rightarrow$ Front-running of global macro-positions.
**[Reaction]** $\rightarrow$ "Gap-risk" increases; classical regime detectors enter a feedback loop of failure, exacerbating volatility.

### D. Cryptographic Trust Collapse
**[Event]** $\rightarrow$ Sudden failure of a major Root Certificate Authority (CA) due to quantum attack.
**[Mechanism]** $\rightarrow$ Total loss of authentication for digital assets and API-driven trading $\rightarrow$ Immediate halt of automated market making (AMM).
**[Reaction]** $\rightarrow$ Total liquidity evaporation; transition to manual/analog settlement modes; extreme price gaps.

## 5. Convergence Synthesis: The "Quantum-Macro" Feedback Loop
The convergence creates a dangerous feedback loop:
1. **Detection:** A quantum-advantaged actor detects a macro-regime shift.
2. **Action:** They execute a massive position change using PQC-shielded channels.
3. **Observation:** Classical models see the price movement but cannot identify the cause (as the signal is obscured by PQC-noise).
4. **Panic:** Classical actors interpret the movement as an exogenous shock or "black swan," triggering a volatility cascade.

## 6. Strategic Mitigation for Infrastructure
To decouple quantum breakthroughs from macro-instability, infrastructure must:
- **Implement Crypto-Agility:** Allow for the rapid swap of primitives without downtime.
- **Hybridize Signatures:** Use both classical and PQC signatures during the transition to prevent "single point of failure" shocks.
- **Synchronize Global Standards:** Reduce the "migration gap" between G7 and EM economies to prevent liquidity fragmentation.
