# World Models & Spatial Intelligence

World Models are AI systems designed to simulate the physical environment, enabling agents to predict the evolution of a scene over time. This capability is the foundation of **Spatial Intelligence**, allowing AI to move beyond static pattern recognition to active spatial reasoning and planning.

## Key Breakthroughs (2026)

### NVIDIA Cosmos
- **Function**: World Foundation Models (WFMs) that generate high-fidelity synthetic data for robot training.
- **Impact**: Bridges the "embodied data bottleneck" by simulating millions of task variations, reducing the need for expensive real-world data collection.
- **Capabilities**: Dynamic view synthesis and advanced navigation.

### Spatial Intelligence Paradigm
- **Shift**: Moving from chatbots to "Physical AI" where intelligence is context-aware and signal-sensing.
- **Generalization**: Systems now learn to generalize across different physical environments rather than being programmed for specific, repetitive motions.

## The "Embodied Data" Bottleneck
Unlike LLMs, which have the internet as a corpus, Physical AI requires data on forces, collisions, and kinematics. 
- **Synthetic Data**: Generated via World Models (e.g., NVIDIA Cosmos).
- **Teleoperation**: Capturing human movement via remote control to create high-quality action datasets.

## Integration: Planning vs. Execution
Modern Physical AI typically employs a hybrid architecture:
1. **World Models (Planning)**: Used for spatial reasoning, predicting outcomes, and strategic planning (e.g., World Labs' Marble).
2. **VLA Models (Execution)**: Vision-Language-Action models (e.g., $\pi_0.6$) that translate plans and visual input directly into motor commands.
