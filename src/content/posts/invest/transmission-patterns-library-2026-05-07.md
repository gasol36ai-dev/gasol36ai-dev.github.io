# Macro-to-Asset Transmission Patterns Library
## Last Updated: 2026-05-07

## 1. Real Yields → Gold
- **Mechanism**: Gold has no yield; competes with yield-bearing assets
- **Transmission**: Rising real yields increase opportunity cost of holding gold → gold prices fall
- **Correlation**: Historically -0.82 between 10Y TIPS and gold prices
- **Current Context**: 
  - US 10Y TIPS yield ~1.8% (nominal 4.39% - breakeven inflation ~2.6%)
  - Gold sensitivity highest when real yields 1-2%
  - Breakdown risk: During extreme geopolitical risk, gold may decouple as safe haven
- **Signal**: Monitor 10Y TIPS yield and breakeven inflation rates

## 2. USD Index → Emerging Markets
- **Mechanism**: EM debt often USD-denominated; commodity exports priced in USD
- **Transmission**: 
  - Stronger USD → higher debt service costs for EM borrowers → capital outflows
  - Stronger USD → weaker commodity prices → hurts EM exporters
  - Stronger USD → makes EM assets more expensive in local currency terms
- **Correlation**: Historically negative between DXY and EM equities/bonds
- **Current Context**:
  - Fed on hold vs ECB BoJ divergent policies supporting USD strength
  - EM Asia (Taiwan, Korea) benefiting from tech cycle may show resilience
  - Latin America more vulnerable to USD strength and commodity dependence
- **Signal**: Monitor DXY, EMBI spreads, commodity prices (copper, oil)

## 3. US Term Structure → Bank Profitability → Credit Growth
- **Mechanism**: Banks borrow short, lend long; NII = spread between loan yields and deposit costs
- **Transmission**:
  - Steepening curve (long yields rising faster than short) → widens NIM → boosts bank profits → increases lending capacity
  - Flattening/inversion → compresses NIM → reduces bank profitability → tightens credit standards
- **Current Context**:
  - 10Y-2Y spread +0.48% and steepening → supportive for bank lending
  - However, regulatory constraints and loan demand remain key variables
- **Signal**: Monitor yield curve shape, bank lending surveys, credit growth metrics

## 4. Inflation Expectations → Currency Strength
- **Mechanism**: Higher expected inflation erodes real returns on currency holdings
- **Transmission**:
  - Rising inflation expectations → currency depreciation pressure (unless offset by higher nominal rates)
  - Anchored inflation expectations → currency stability
- **Current Context**:
  - US inflation expectations rising (3.2% forecast) vs Euro area falling (1.9%)
  - Supporting USD strength vs EUR weakness
  - Real interest rate differentials driving forex flows
- **Signal**: Monitor breakeven inflation rates, central bank credibility surveys, forex forwards

## 5. Geopolitical Risk → Safe Haven Flows
- **Mechanism**: Risk-off sentiment increases demand for perceived safe assets
- **Transmission**:
  - Escalating geopolitical tension → flight to quality → increased demand for USD, Treasuries, gold, CHF, JPY
  - De-escalation → risk-on → capital flows back to risk assets (EM equities, HY bonds, commodities)
- **Current Context**:
  - Middle East tensions (Strait of Hormuz) creating energy supply risk premium
  - Supporting gold and safe haven demand despite positive real yields
  - Creating stagflationary pressure (higher inflation + growth concerns)
- **Signal**: Monitor geopolitical risk indices, oil prices, shipping costs, volatility indices (VIX, GVZ)

## 6. Tech Export Momentum → Currency Strength (Taiwan Specific)
- **Mechanism**: Strong export demand increases foreign currency inflows, putting upward pressure on local currency
- **Transmission**:
  - AI-driven semiconductor demand → increased Taiwan exports → TWD appreciation pressure
  - Strong TWD → hurts competitiveness of other Taiwanese exporters → potential policy response
  - Capital inflows from foreign investment in Taiwanese tech assets
- **Current Context**:
  - Taiwan GDP forecast upgraded to 7.56% on AI export strength
  - Goods exports forecast 27.11% growth in 2026
  - Potential for TWD strength to create monetary policy dilemma for CBC
- **Signal**: Monitor Taiwan export data, semiconductor sales (TSMC guidance), TWD/USD, central bank intervention

## 7. Central Bank Policy Divergence → Capital Flows
- **Mechanism**: Interest rate differentials drive carry trades and portfolio allocation
- **Transmission**:
  - Higher rates in Currency A vs Currency B → capital flows from B to A seeking yield
  - Expectations of future policy changes amplify current differentials
  - Hedging costs and risk appetite modulate the flow magnitude
- **Current Context**:
  - Fed on hold (5.25-5.50%) vs ECB (deposit facility 2.50%) vs BoJ (-0.10%)
  - Creating significant USD funding advantage vs EUR and JPY
  - Supporting USD strength and carry trade popularity
- **Signal**: Monitor policy rate differentials, forward rate agreements, cross-currency basis swaps, fund flow data

## Usage Notes
- These patterns are probabilistic, not deterministic
- Multiple mechanisms often operate simultaneously
- Regime changes can alter historical correlations
- Always consider countervailing forces and timing lags
- Best used as part of a broader mosaic of indicators
