# Research Report: Macro-Transmission Cascades (June 2026)

## Executive Summary
Macro-Transmission Cascades in 2026 have evolved from linear, rate-sensitive adjustments to non-linear, structural "step-function" shifts. The global economy has transitioned from a **Rate-Driven Era** to a **Systemic Insurance Era**, where traditional monetary signals (e.g., Fed funds rate) are frequently overridden by structural dependencies in the "Sovereign Stack" (Compute, Energy, and Minerals) and geopolitical risk premiums. The defining characteristic of current cascades is their cross-domain nature, where a shock in a foundational physical layer (e.g., Critical Minerals) triggers a non-linear re-rating of sovereign financial assets.

## 1. Key Breakthroughs & Findings

### 1.1. The "Sovereign Stack" Dependency
Research indicates that intelligence production is now treated as a vertically integrated industrial process. A bottleneck at any layer—Minerals $\rightarrow$ Fabrication $\rightarrow$ Logic Design $\rightarrow$ Model Training—does not merely raise costs but triggers a **Procurement Cascade**, forcing states to vertically integrate the entire stack to eliminate foreign "kill switches."

### 1.2. Correlation Decoupling (The Insurance Regime)
A critical finding is the breakdown of the Gold/Real Yield inverse correlation. In the current regime, Gold and Real Yields have shown a positive correlation (+0.18), signaling that Gold is no longer just a hedge against inflation but a **Systemic Insurance** asset against sovereign trust erosion and geopolitical collapse.

### 1.3. Non-Linear Sovereignty Re-rating
The identification of "Sovereignty Gates" (e.g., PASG, TSCG) proves that when a nation-state simultaneously crosses independence thresholds in three or more critical vectors (e.g., CBDC + Nuclear SMR + Robotics), it triggers a non-linear expansion of the **Sovereign Premium**. Markets systematically underweight these compounding effects, pricing each layer in isolation until a "gate" fires, causing a sudden asset re-rating.

### 1.4. Cognitive Liquidity Shifts
The rise of AI-native microstructure has introduced "Cognitive Voids," where traditional human-driven liquidity signals lose predictive power. Liquidity cascades are now driven by agentic regime detection, where AI agents autonomously rebalance portfolios based on "LOB Toxicity" and "RWA Yield Geometries" before human traders can react.

## 3. Implications for Global Economic Policy & Stability

### 3.1. End of Geopolitical Terrestrialism
The shift toward **Orbital and Cryptographic Autonomy** suggests that sovereign power is no longer bound by terrestrial geography or fiber-optic cables. Policy stability will increasingly depend on the ability to maintain "Multi-Substrate Sovereignty," rendering traditional sanctions (like SWIFT) obsolete as CBDCs and Orbital DePINs provide alternative settlement and communication rails.

### 3.2. Policy Paralysis and the Stagflationary Trap
Central banks are facing a "Double Bind" where structural inflation (driven by energy and trade wars) conflicts with growth deceleration (driven by decoupling). This creates a regime of **Policy Impotence**, where "Higher for Longer" is a necessity regardless of GDP data, increasing the risk of systemic debt crises in emerging markets.

### 3.3. Transition to "Just-in-Case" Economics
The global economy is moving from *Just-in-Time* efficiency to *Just-in-Case* resilience. This involves the conversion of foreign veto points into domestic capex line items. While this increases national security, it permanently raises the baseline cost of production, ensuring that inflation remains "sticky" and higher than the pre-2020 era.

### 3.4. Systemic Fragility: The "Neuro-Flash Crash"
The dominance of AI-native agents in the liquidity layer introduces the risk of **Non-Linear Market Cascades**. If multiple agentic models detect the same regime shift simultaneously, they may trigger instantaneous, un-moderated cross-chain cascades, leading to "Cognitive Voids" where price discovery fails entirely for short durations.
