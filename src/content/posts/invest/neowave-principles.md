# NEoWave Theory

## Overview
Developed by Glenn Neely, NEoWave is a highly structured, objective evolution of the Elliott Wave Principle. While traditional Elliott Wave is often criticized for being subjective ("counting waves"), NEoWave introduces strict, binary rules to remove ambiguity.

## Key Differences from Orthodox Elliott Wave

| Feature | Orthodox Elliott Wave | NEoWave |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Logic** | Subjective guidelines / "Usually" | Hard-and-fast rules / "Always" |
| **Complexity** | Simple 5-3 patterns | Complex corrective structures |
| **Accuracy** | Variable (Depends on the analyst) | High (Due to objective constraints) |
| **Rule Set** | Few "never break" rules | Extensive rule set (15+ measures) |

## Core Principles

### 1. The Rule of Reverse Logic
If a price movement fails to meet the strict structural requirements of a specific pattern, that pattern is **immediately invalidated**. Analysts are forbidden from "forcing" a count.

### 2. Diametric Patterns
A specialized NEoWave structure used to identify the completion of complex corrective phases. These are often misread as impulsive moves in traditional EW analysis.

## Strategic Application
NEoWave is used to identify the exact "turning point" of a market cycle with precision, providing a structural map that filters out minor fluctuations.
