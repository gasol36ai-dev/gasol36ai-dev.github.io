# Resource-Strategic Fragility Gate (RSFG)

## Overview
The RSFG is a high-fidelity indicator designed to detect the onset of "Resource Weaponization" and "Supply Chain Fragmentation" cycles. It identifies when geopolitical tensions transition from rhetorical threats to actual structural disruptions in the physical flow of critical commodities.

## 🛠️ Map-Trigger-Lock Framework

### 1. Map (The Observables)
The indicator synthesizes three distinct data streams:
- **Commodity Basis Spreads**: The divergence between spot prices and long-dated futures for critical resources (Energy, Rare Earths, Grains, Semiconductors). A widening spread indicates physical scarcity or hoarding.
- **Geopolitical Risk Premium (GRP)**: Quantified volatility in energy and mineral sectors specifically linked to geopolitical event triggers (e.g., Hormuz tension, trade sanction announcements).
- **Logistics Congestion/Variance**: The second derivative of lead times and shipping costs in strategic corridors (e.g., Suez, Panama, Malacca Straits).

### 2. Trigger (The Signal)
The RSFG signal fires when:
`[Basis Spread Expansion > 2σ] AND [GRP Acceleration > 15% WoW] AND [Logistics Lead-Time Variance > 20% YoY]`

### 3. Lock (The Reaction)
Upon a confirmed RSFG signal, the following automated execution protocols are triggered:
- **Asset Reallocation**: Systematic reduction of exposure to "Global Integration" sectors (Multinational Tech, Automotive, Global Consumer Goods) by 25%.
- **Strategic Pivot**: Immediate increase in allocation to "Domestic Resource Resilience" sectors (Mining, Energy Infrastructure, Defense, Domestic Agricultural Inputs).
- **Risk Buffer**: Deployment of a 15% cash reserve increase to accommodate higher volatility in the transition period.

## 🎯 Strategic Value
The RSFG provides an early warning for the transition from a "Global Efficiency" regime to a "National Security/Resilience" regime. Unlike simple inflation indicators, RSFG focuses on the **structural ability to supply**, making it a superior predictor of stagflationary shocks and supply-side ruptures.
