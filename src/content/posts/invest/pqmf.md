# Concept: Post-Quantum Macro-Financials (PQMF)

## Definition
Post-Quantum Macro-Financials (PQMF) is the study and management of macroeconomic stability and financial system integrity during the transition from classical asymmetric cryptography (RSA, ECC) to Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC). It focuses on the systemic risks posed by the "Cryptographic Breakpoint"—the moment a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) can break current encryption standards—and the subsequent impact on sovereign trust, monetary policy, and global asset valuation.

## Core Pillars
1. **Cryptographic Sovereignty**: The ability of a state to protect its monetary issuance and debt obligations against quantum-adversarial decryption.
2. **Migration Velocity**: The speed at which financial infrastructures (CBDCs, SWIFT, clearing houses) transition to PQC algorithms.
3. **Quantum-Threat Premium**: The additional yield or risk premium demanded by investors for assets whose underlying security is not yet quantum-resistant.

## Transmission Mapping Framework
PQMF utilizes **Transmission Mapping** to analyze systemic shocks:
`[Event] -> [Mechanism] -> [Reaction]`

- **Event**: The trigger (e.g., announcement of a 10k-qubit stable processor).
- **Mechanism**: The technical or psychological channel (e.g., perceived vulnerability of sovereign bond signatures).
- **Reaction**: The macroeconomic outcome (e.g., capital flight to "quantum-safe" jurisdictions).

## Key Metrics
- **Q-Exposure Index**: Percentage of total sovereign debt secured by non-PQC signatures.
- **PQC-Migration Gap**: Time difference between the projected CRQC arrival and the completion of financial sector PQC deployment.
- **Quantum Volatility Coefficient**: Sensitivity of asset prices to quantum-computing breakthroughs.
