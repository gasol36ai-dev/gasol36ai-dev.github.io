# Macro Intelligence Report - 2026-05-17

## Executive Summary
The global macro landscape has shifted significantly from a "disinflation/rate-cut" regime to a "re-inflation/rate-hike" regime, primarily driven by geopolitical instability in the Middle East and subsequent energy price shocks. Central banks (Fed, ECB, BoJ) are all adjusting their stances toward a more hawkish orientation.

## 1. Central Bank Policy & Interest Rates
* **Federal Reserve (Fed):** Market expectations have pivoted. Following a hotter-than-expected April CPI (3.8%) and PPI (6.0%), the probability of a rate hike by year-end has risen to ~30-37%. The debate has moved from "when to cut" to "is a hike necessary?" The incoming Chair Kevin Warsh faces a difficult balancing act between political pressure for cuts and the reality of inflation.
* **European Central Bank (ECB):** Despite economic fragility, the ECB is signaling a potential rate hike in June to combat energy-led inflation risks. The deposit rate is currently 2.00%.
* **Bank of Japan (BoJ):** Signaling a possible rate hike as early as next month, driven by upside inflation risks from the Middle East conflict.

## 2. Inflation & Energy
* **US Inflation:** CPI rose 0.6% MoM (3.8% YoY). Core CPI (2.8%) remains well above the 2% target. Energy costs (Oil > $100/bbl) are the primary driver.
* **Energy Shock:** The conflict involving Iran has pushed Brent crude above $108 and WTI above $104. This energy-driven inflation is creating "second-round" risks as importers hike costs.

## 3. Sovereign Bond Yields
* **US Treasury Yields:** Spiking across the curve. 10Y yield surged toward 4.60%, and 2Y yield is near 4.08%, reflecting the repricing of Fed policy.
* **Global Context:** Yields are rising globally, with German Bunds (10Y at 3.127%) and UK Gilts (4.56%) also seeing upward pressure.

## 4. Geopolitical Risk
* **Middle East Conflict:** The ongoing tension involving Iran remains the primary systemic risk, threatening global energy supplies (Strait of Hormuz) and driving volatility in both commodities and sovereign debt.

## 5. Macro-to-Asset Transmission Patterns
* **Energy Shock $\rightarrow$ Inflation $\uparrow$ $\rightarrow$ Central Bank Hawkishness $\rightarrow$ Bond Yields $\uparrow$ $\rightarrow$ Equities $\downarrow$ & Gold (Volatility Dependent).**
* **High Real Yields $\rightarrow$ Pressure on Gold** (though geopolitical risk may provide a floor).
* **USD Strength (due to higher rates) $\rightarrow$ Pressure on Emerging Markets.**

## 6. Macro Watchlist (High Impact)
1. **Crude Oil Prices (Brent/WTI):** Critical driver of headline inflation.
2. **US Core CPI/PPI:** Key indicator for Fed's next move.
3. **US 10Y Treasury Yield:** Benchmark for global discount rates.
4. **Middle East Geopolitical Escalation:** Source of systemic volatility and energy shocks.
5. **Fed Policy Pivot (Hike vs. Hold):** Directional driver for all risk assets.
