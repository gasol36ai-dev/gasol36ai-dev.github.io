## Macro Reconnaissance Summary (May 2026)

### 1. Central Bank Policy Divergence
- **Fed (US)**: Maintaining a restrictive stance ("higher for longer") under the new leadership. Policy is data-dependent, with inflation (PCE 3.2%, Core 3.0% in March) still a concern.
- **ECB (Eurozone)**: Facing upwardly revised inflation (2.6% in March) amid economic stagnation. Positioned with a hike bias to combat energy-driven inflation.
- **BoJ (Japan)**: Moving into a normalization phase, stepping out of the shadow of the Fed/ECB. This is a primary driver for global carry-trade unwind and volatility.

### 2. Inflation & Energy Shock
- **Geopolitical Trigger**: Ongoing tensions in the Middle East/Strait of Hormuz are impacting oil supply (~20% of global supply).
- **Oil Prices**: Brent crude is seeing upward pressure ($85+), contributing to headline inflation risks.
- **Inflation Regime**: Transitioning from disinflationary to a regime where energy shocks drive structural inflation.

### 3. Yield Dynamics
- **US 10Y Yield**: Hovering around 4.36-4.45%.
- **Curve Signal**: The 10Y-2Y spread (~50bps) indicates a normalization phase (transition from recession fears to inflation/term-premium regime).
- **Real Yields vs Gold**: The traditional decoupling is confirmed; gold is rising alongside yields due to structural demand (central bank buying, de-dollarization, geopolitical hedging).

### 4. Geopolitical Risk
- **Middle East**: High risk of sustained energy supply disruptions.
- **Systemic Trust**: US credit rating risk (potential Moody's downgrade) continues to add a permanent risk premium to Treasuries.
