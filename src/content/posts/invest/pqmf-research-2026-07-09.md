# Research Report: Post-Quantum Macro-Financials (PQMF) Trends (2025-2026)
**Date**: 2026-07-09
**Focus**: CBDCs, Sovereign Debt, and Volatility Modeling
**Framework**: Transmission Mapping ([Event] -> [Mechanism] -> [Reaction])

## 1. Executive Summary
The 2025-2026 period marks the "Critical Migration Window." As NIST PQC standards move from specification to deep integration, the global financial system faces a bifurcated reality: jurisdictions that achieve "Quantum Sovereignty" and those remaining exposed to "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" (HNDL) risks. The emergence of PQMF as a discipline reflects the transition of quantum threats from a technical IT concern to a core macroeconomic stability risk.

## 2. Impact on Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs)
CBDCs represent the most vulnerable yet most critical point of failure. Unlike traditional ledger systems, the programmable nature of CBDCs relies on cryptographic primitives for issuance, validation, and privacy.

### Transmission Maps for CBDCs:
- **[Event: CRQC Breakthrough] -> [Mechanism: Forgery of Minting Keys] -> [Reaction: Hyper-inflationary shock via unauthorized currency creation].**
- **[Event: PQC Migration Delay] -> [Mechanism: Loss of 'Finality' Trust] -> [Reaction: Transition of CBDC from 'Safe Haven' to 'Risk Asset', leading to massive outflows to physical gold/commodities].**
- **[Event: Implementation of Lattice-based Signatures] -> [Mechanism: Increased Transaction Latency/Compute Overhead] -> [Reaction: Reduced throughput in retail payments, creating friction in high-velocity economic zones].**

### Strategic Trend: The "Quantum-Safe Ledger" Race
Central banks are shifting from "Hybrid" (Classical + PQC) to "Pure PQC" architectures. The 2026 trend is the adoption of **Stateful Hash-Based Signatures (LMS/XMSS)** for root-of-trust in CBDC issuance to ensure long-term immutability.

## 3. Sovereign Debt Stability during Quantum Transition
Sovereign bonds are fundamentally promises of future payment, secured by digital signatures. The transition to PQC introduces "Quantum Credit Risk."

### Transmission Maps for Sovereign Debt:
- **[Event: Leak of Non-PQC Bond Registry] -> [Mechanism: HNDL (Harvest Now, Decrypt Later) revelation of secret ownership/collateral] -> [Reaction: Sudden liquidation and market panic].**
- **[Event: Divergent PQC Adoption Rates] -> [Mechanism: Creation of 'Quantum-Safe' vs 'Quantum-Exposed' bond tiers] -> [Reaction: Yield divergence; quantum-exposed bonds suffer a 'Security Discount' (Quantum-Threat Premium)].**
- **[Event: Quantum-enabled Cryptanalysis of Debt Swaps] -> [Mechanism: Exposure of hidden derivative positions] -> [Reaction: Systemic contagion as counterparty risks are revealed in real-time].**

### Analysis: The Sovereign Quantum Gap
We observe a growing "Quantum Divide." G7 nations are integrating PQC into treasury systems, while emerging markets struggle. This creates a new form of geopolitical leverage where "Quantum-Safe" currencies act as the new global reserve anchors.

## 4. New Volatility Models for Quantum-Threat Eras
Classical volatility models (GARCH, Black-Scholes) assume continuous price movements. Quantum threats introduce "Step-Function Volatility"—instantaneous, discrete jumps based on binary breakthroughs.

### Proposed Model: The Quantum-Event Volatility (QEV) Framework
The QEV framework replaces standard variance with a **Probability-Impact Matrix of Cryptographic Collapse**.

**Transmission Map for Volatility:**
- **[Event: Qubit Stability Milestone] -> [Mechanism: Shift in 'Expected Time to Break' (ETB) estimate] -> [Reaction: Instantaneous repricing of all encrypted assets (The 'Quantum Spike')].**

### Key Model Components:
1. **Cryptographic Shock Index (CSI)**: A real-time metric measuring the percentage of global liquidity secured by vulnerable algorithms.
2. **The "Y2Q" Countdown Variable**: A time-decay function that increases volatility as the projected date of the Cryptographic Breakpoint approaches.
3. **Entropy-Based Hedge Ratios**: New hedging strategies using "Quantum-Neutral" assets (e.g., physical assets or PQC-verified synthetic derivatives).

## 5. Conclusion and Policy Recommendations
The financial system is currently in a state of "Cryptographic Fragility." To mitigate the risks identified in the PQMF framework, the following is recommended:
- **Mandatory PQC-Audit for Sovereign Issuers**: Standardizing a "Quantum-Readiness Score" for national debts.
- **Interoperable PQC-Bridges**: Ensuring that different PQC standards (e.g., Kyber vs. Dilithium) can communicate across borders to prevent liquidity fragmentation.
- **Quantum-Volatility Buffers**: Requiring systemic banks to hold capital reserves specifically against "Cryptographic Shock" events.
