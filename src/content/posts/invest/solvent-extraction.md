# Solvent Extraction

## Core Concept
Solvent Extraction (SX) is a chemical separation process that uses two immiscible liquids (typically an aqueous phase and an organic phase) to selectively transfer specific solutes from one phase to the other based on their relative solubilities.

## Technical Advantages
- **High Selectivity**: Can be tuned to separate extremely similar chemical species (e.g., rare earth elements) using specific organic extractants.
- **Scalability**: Well-established industrial-scale technology suitable for continuous, large-volume processing.
- **Modular Implementation**: Can be deployed in various configurations (mixer-settlers, centrifugal contactors) to meet specific throughput and purity requirements.

## Strategic Value
Solvent Extraction is a critical component of **Critical Mineral Sovereignty**, enabling the high-purity recovery of essential elements (e.g., Lithium, Cobalt, Neodymium) from complex ores or recycled streams.

## Transmission Mapping
[Solute in Feed Phase] $\rightarrow$ [Selective Phase Transfer (Extraction)] $\rightarrow$ [High-Purity Solute in Extract Phase]

## Operational/Deployment SOPs
1. **Extractant Selection**: Choose organic extractants with high selectivity and stability for the target chemical species.
2. **Phase Contact Optimization**: Optimize mixer-settler or centrifugal contactor parameters to maximize mass transfer and phase separation efficiency.
3. **Continuous Flow Management**: Implement robust continuous flow control to ensure consistent extraction efficiency and high-purity output.

## Core Logic/Formalization
- **Distribution Coefficient**: $D = \frac{[C]_{\text{organic}}}{[C]_{\text{aqueous}}}$ (where $[C]$ is the concentration of the solute).
- **Extraction Efficiency**: $E = \frac{D}{D + (V_{\text{aq}}/V_{\text{org}})}$ (where $V_{\text{aq}}$ and $V_{\text{org}}$ are the volumes of the aqueous and organic phases).
