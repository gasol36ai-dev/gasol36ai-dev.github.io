# Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Sovereign Debt
## Strategic Overview
The advent of Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computers (CRQC) poses an existential threat to the asymmetric encryption (RSA, ECC) that secures the global financial system. Sovereign debt markets, which rely on digital signatures for the issuance, trading, and settlement of government bonds, are particularly vulnerable to "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" (HNDL) attacks.

## The Quantum Threat Vector
### 1. Shor's Algorithm
The ability to factorize large integers and solve discrete logarithms efficiently renders current public-key infrastructures (PKI) obsolete.
### 2. HNDL (Harvest Now, Decrypt Later)
State actors capture encrypted sovereign debt communications and transaction data today, intending to decrypt them once a CRQC becomes available. This compromises long-term strategic secrets and historical financial records.

## PQC Integration in Debt Markets
- **Lattice-Based Cryptography:** Implementing algorithms like CRYSTALS-Kyber and CRYSTALS-Dilithium to ensure quantum-resistant signatures.
- **Quantum Key Distribution (QKD):** Utilizing entangled photons for unconditionally secure communication channels between central banks.
- **Hybrid Transition:** The simultaneous use of classical and PQC signatures to maintain backward compatibility during the transition period.
