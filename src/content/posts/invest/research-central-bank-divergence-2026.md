# Central Bank Divergence (May 2026)

The global monetary landscape in May 2026 is characterized by a sharp divergence in policy trajectories between the three major central banks, driven by varying sensitivities to energy shocks and inflation.

## Policy Profiles

### 1. Federal Reserve (Fed)
- **Stance**: Data-dependent / Patient.
- **Trajectory**: Projecting 2 rate cuts in 2026, but shifting toward "on hold longer" as headline inflation spikes.
- **Key Driver**: Balancing core inflation persistence against GDP growth.
- **Yield Signal**: 10Y-2Y spread has turned positive (~50bps), signaling a transition from recession fears to a term-premium/inflation regime.

### 2. European Central Bank (ECB)
- **Stance**: Hawkish / Inflation-Fighting.
- **Trajectory**: Bias toward rate hikes (~50bps projected in 2026).
- **Key Driver**: Extreme vulnerability to energy supply shocks (e.g., Strait of Hormuz risks) causing headline inflation to surge.
- ** transmission**: [Energy Shock] $\rightarrow$ [$\uparrow$ Headline CPI] $\rightarrow$ [$\uparrow$ ECB Rates].

### 3. Bank of Japan (BoJ)
- **Stance**: Normalization Phase.
- **Trajectory**: Steady rates (0.75%) but revising inflation forecasts upwards (2.8%).
- **Key Driver**: Ending the "frozen era"; transitioning to a positive corporate behavior environment.
- **Risk**: BoJ normalization remains a primary trigger for global equity volatility and carry trade unwinding.

## Strategic Implications
- **USD Strength**: The Fed's relative patience compared to the ECB's hike bias and BoJ's cautiousness generally supports a relatively strong USD, though structural pressures (devaluation narratives) create a ceiling.
- **Capital Flows**: Divergence in real yields encourages rotation from Euro-denominated assets toward USD-denominated assets during energy-led volatility.
