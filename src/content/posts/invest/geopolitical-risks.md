# Geopolitical Risk Landscape 2026
## Overview
Geopolitical fragmentation is no longer a peripheral risk but a core driver of macro volatility, supply chain reconfiguration, and the emergence of multi-polar financial systems.

## Primary Vectors
1. **Sovereign Tech Competition**: The race for AI supremacy, semiconductor sovereignty, and quantum advantage is driving aggressive industrial policies and export controls.
2. **Resource Nationalism**: Protectionist policies regarding critical minerals (Lithium, Cobalt, REEs, and HALEU) are creating new geopolitical leverage points.
3. **Digital and Information Sovereignty**: The rise of localized internet, data governance regimes, and cognitive security defense measures.
4. **Maritime and Orbital Chokepoints**: Competition over physical transit routes (Suez, Malacca) and orbital logistics/space infrastructure.

## Impact on Markets
- **Commodity Risk Premia**: Increased volatility in energy, metals, and agricultural markets due to supply chain fragility.
- **Supply Chain Redundancy Costs**: The shift from "Just-in-Time" to "Just-in-Case" (friend-shoring/near-shoring) is driving structural inflation.
- **Fragmentation of Capital Flows**: Decoupling of Western and Eastern financial architectures, including the emergence of alternative clearing and settlement systems.
- **Asset Re-rating**: Shift in risk premiums for sovereign debt in geopolitically sensitive regions.

## Monitoring Framework
- **Geopolitical Risk Matrix (GRM)**: Mapping vector intensity against market impact.
- **Supply Chain Fragility Index (SCFI)**: Measuring reliance on single-source/single-region providers for critical technologies.
- **Sovereign Autonomy Score**: Quantifying a nation's ability to maintain policy independence in a fragmented world.
